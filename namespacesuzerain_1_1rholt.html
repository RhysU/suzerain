Detailed Description

Provides compute kernels for formulations using a reference density \(\rho_0\), length \(l_0\), and temperature \(T_0\).

The ideal gas equation of state is employed:

\begin{align*} p &= \left(\gamma-1\right) \left( e - \frac{m\cdot{}m}{2\rho} \right) \\ T &= \gamma{} \frac{p}{\rho} \\ \mu &= T^{\beta} \\ \lambda &= \left(\alpha - \frac{2}{3}\right) \mu \end{align*}

The following nondimensional variable definitions are used:

  • \(p\) or p is pressure.
  • \(T\) or T is temperature.
  • \(\vec{u}\) or u is the velocity vector.
  • \(\rho\) or rho is density.
  • \(\vec{m}\) or m is the momentum vector \(\rho\vec{u}\).
  • \(e\) or e is the total energy \(\rho\tilde{e}\) where \(\tilde{e}\) is the sum of the internal and kinetic energy densities.
  • \(\mu\) or mu is the first viscosity.
  • \(\lambda\) or lambda is the second viscosity.
  • \(\alpha\) or alpha is the scaling factor relating \(\lambda\) and \( \mu \).
  • \(\beta\) or beta is the constant coefficient in the power viscosity law.
  • \(\gamma\) or gamma is the constant ratio of specific heats \(C_p/C_v\).

Templated Scalar, Vector, and Tensor types are used. These functions are intended to be used with Eigen's vector and matrix types. For example, when Scalar == double Vector and Tensor may be Eigen::Matrix<Scalar,3,1> and Eigen::Matrix<Scalar,3,3>, respectively.
